7 Cures for a Lean Purse

Summary: Arkad teaches seven remedies to fill an empty purse: (1) start thy purse to fattening (save at least 10%), (2) control thy expenditures (budget), (3) make thy gold multiply (invest), (4) guard thy treasures from loss (seek safe advice), (5) make of thy dwelling a profitable investment (own home), (6) insure a future income (plan for old age/family), and (7) increase thy ability to earn (learn and grow).

Alignment to my life: As a student, I can apply the first cure by saving a small portion of any allowance or part-time earnings. The second cure helps me track my spending on textbooks, food, and entertainment. The seventh cure is most relevant: I can increase my earning ability by learning new skills, which directly improves my academic performance and future job prospects.

Biggest takeaway: Small, consistent actions—like saving a tenth of what I earn—build lasting wealth. It is not about how much I make, but how I manage it.

5 Laws of Gold

Summary: The five laws are: (1) Gold comes gladly to those who save at least a tenth, (2) Gold works diligently for the wise owner who invests it, (3) Gold clings to the cautious owner who seeks advice, (4) Gold slips away from those who invest in unfamiliar businesses, and (5) Gold flees from those who chase impossible earnings or follow tricksters.

Alignment to my life: The third law reminds me to seek mentors and advisors before making big decisions—whether about my studies or finances. The fourth law warns me not to jump into things I don't understand. As a student, I should focus on mastering my field rather than chasing shortcuts.

Biggest takeaway: Wisdom and caution are more valuable than gold itself. Following these laws can turn small savings into a secure future.

Overall Reflection

Reading "The Richest Man in Babylon" was surprisingly practical. The parables are simple but profound. The idea of "paying yourself first" is something I can start doing today, even with a small income. The book also taught me that financial success is not about luck—it's about discipline, learning, and seeking good advice. My biggest takeaway is that wealth is built slowly and wisely, and that I have the power to change my financial habits now.
